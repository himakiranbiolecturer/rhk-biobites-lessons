01

Chapter map

Growth changes quantity; differentiation changes structure and function; development includes both.

GROWTH

Permanent increase

An irreversible increase in size of an organ, part or cell, usually accompanied by metabolism and increased protoplasm.

DIFFERENTIATION

Specialisation

Cells mature with structural and biochemical changes suited to particular functions.

DEVELOPMENT

Life-cycle change

Includes all changes from germination to senescence. Broadly: growth + differentiation, influenced by genes, PGRs and environment.

02

Features and measurement of growth

Plants retain meristems, giving them an open and often indeterminate growth pattern.

Primary growth

Root and shoot apical meristems add length along the plant axis.

Secondary growth

Vascular cambium and cork cambium increase girth, especially in dicots and gymnosperms.

Measured indirectly

Use fresh/dry mass, length, area, volume or cell number because protoplasmic increase is difficult to measure directly.

Conditions

Water enables enlargement and enzyme activity; oxygen supplies respiratory energy; nutrients build protoplasm. Temperature, light and gravity also matter.

Open growth

Meristems self-perpetuate while their derivatives usually lose division capacity and form the plant body.

Growth ≠ imbibition

Swelling of dry wood in water is reversible physical uptake, not permanent biological growth.

05

Differentiation and plasticity

Plant cell fate remains unusually flexible and strongly depends on position and environment.

DIFFERENTIATION

Mature for function

A tracheary element loses protoplasm and develops strong lignocellulosic secondary walls for water conduction under tension.

DEDIFFERENTIATION

Regain division

Living mature cells become meristematic again—for example, parenchyma forming interfascicular cambium or cork cambium.

REDIFFERENTIATION

Specialise again

Cells produced by a dedifferentiated meristem later lose division capacity and mature into new functional tissues.

PLASTICITY

Different developmental routes

Plants form different structures in response to age or environment. Heterophylly is a classic example.

Developmental heterophylly

Juvenile and adult leaves differ in cotton, coriander and larkspur.

Environmental heterophylly

Buttercup produces different leaf forms in air and water.

08

High-yield NEET traps

Small distinctions that frequently decide the answer.

Arithmetic versus geometric

Arithmetic: one daughter keeps dividing and the other differentiates. Geometric: both daughters retain division capacity.

AGR versus RGR

AGR is total increase per time; RGR expresses increase per time relative to initial size.

Sigmoid is natural

Exponential growth cannot continue indefinitely under limited resources; lag, log and stationary phases give an S-curve.

Kinetin is not natural

It was isolated from autoclaved herring sperm DNA; zeatin is a natural cytokinin.

Ethylene can promote

Although largely inhibitory, it breaks dormancy, promotes deep-water-rice elongation, root hairs and some flowering responses.

Auxin and abscission

Auxin prevents premature drop but may promote abscission of older mature organs.

ABA versus GA

They commonly act antagonistically in seed dormancy and germination.

Development is broader

Development includes germination through senescence—not merely increase in size.

PGR action is contextual

Regulators may act individually, synergistically or antagonistically; no one has only a single effect.

Last-minute revision

Open a checkpoint for rapid recall.

What are the three phases near a root tip?

Meristematic: division, dense protoplasm, prominent nuclei. Elongation: vacuolation, enlargement and new wall deposition. Maturation: maximal size, wall thickening and specialised protoplasm.

What are the key growth equations?

Arithmetic: Lₜ = L₀ + rt. Exponential: W₁ = W₀eʳᵗ. AGR = change/time. RGR = change/(initial size × time), in the simple NCERT comparison sense.

Which PGR should a student associate first with each effect?

Auxin—apical dominance/rooting; GA—bolting; cytokinin—cell division/delayed senescence; ethylene—fruit ripening; ABA—stomatal closure/dormancy.
