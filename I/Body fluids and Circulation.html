Coagulation and lymph

Two linked protective transport processes frequently tested together.

Clotting cascade

From injury to fibrin mesh

Injury & platelet factors
→
Prothrombin → thrombin
→
Fibrinogen → fibrin

Thrombokinase and Ca²⁺ are important; the fibrin network traps formed elements to produce the clot.

Tissue fluid

Formation and return

Water and small solutes leave capillaries while larger proteins and most formed elements remain. Lymphatic vessels collect this interstitial fluid and return it to major veins.

Lacteals in intestinal villi absorb fats; lymph also carries specialised lymphocytes.

Circulatory pathways and human heart

Compare vertebrate plans, then follow the heart’s one-way electrical and mechanical organisation.

2 chambers

Fishes

One atrium and one ventricle; single circulation.

3 chambers

Amphibians and most reptiles

Two atria, one ventricle; incomplete double circulation with some mixing.

4 chambers

Crocodiles, birds and mammals

Two atria and two ventricles; complete separation and double circulation.

Valves and one-way flow

Right AV opening—tricuspid; left AV opening—bicuspid/mitral. Semilunar valves guard pulmonary artery and aorta. Valves prevent backflow.

Ventricular walls are thicker than atrial walls; the left ventricle supplies the high-pressure systemic circuit.

Cardiac regulation and disorders

The heart is myogenic, yet neural and hormonal inputs modify its performance.

Intrinsic

Nodal tissue initiates activity without external stimulation; hence the human heart is myogenic.

Sympathetic

Raises heart rate, ventricular contraction strength and cardiac output.

Parasympathetic

Lowers heart rate, conduction speed and cardiac output. Adrenal medullary hormones can increase output.

Hypertension

Repeated pressure of 140/90 mm Hg or higher in the NCERT description; it affects the heart and organs such as brain and kidneys.

Coronary artery disease

Deposits of calcium, fat, cholesterol and fibrous tissue narrow vessels supplying cardiac muscle.

Angina pectoris

Acute chest pain when insufficient oxygen reaches cardiac muscle due to reduced blood flow.

Heart failure

The heart cannot pump effectively enough for body needs; it is not identical to cardiac arrest or heart attack.

Universal labels need care

“O donor” and “AB recipient” refer to ABO red-cell compatibility; clinical transfusion also requires Rh and cross-matching.

Arteries are directional

They carry blood away from the heart; pulmonary arteries are deoxygenated and pulmonary veins oxygenated.

Last-minute revision

Open each checkpoint for rapid classroom recall.

What are the formed elements and their major roles?

RBCs transport respiratory gases, WBCs contribute to defence and immunity, and platelets release factors important in coagulation.

What are the ECG meanings?

P—atrial depolarisation; QRS—ventricular depolarisation and beginning of ventricular systole; T—ventricular repolarisation, with its end marking the end of systole.

What is the cardiac-output equation?

Cardiac output = heart rate × stroke volume. At 72 beats/min and about 70 mL/beat, it is approximately 5 L/min.

How do pulmonary and systemic circuits differ?

Pulmonary: right ventricle → lungs → left atrium. Systemic: left ventricle → body tissues → right atrium.
