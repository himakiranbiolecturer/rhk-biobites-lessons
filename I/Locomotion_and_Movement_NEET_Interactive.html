Sarcolemma

Plasma membrane surrounding a muscle fibre; it conducts the action potential.

Sarcoplasm

Cytoplasm of muscle fibre containing many nuclei and parallel myofibrils.

Sarcoplasmic reticulum

Specialised endoplasmic reticulum serving as the intracellular Ca²⁺ store.

Red / aerobic fibres

High myoglobin, many mitochondria and strong aerobic ATP production. Their oxygen store gives a reddish appearance.

White fibres

Low myoglobin, fewer mitochondria and more sarcoplasmic reticulum. They rely more strongly on anaerobic energy pathways.

Skull

8 cranial + 14 facial bones. Hyoid is separate; each middle ear has malleus, incus and stapes. Human skull is dicondylic.

Vertebral column

7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, one fused sacral unit and one fused coccygeal unit = 26 adult vertebral units.

Ribs

Pairs 1–7 true, 8–10 vertebrochondral/false, and 11–12 floating. Each rib is bicephalic dorsally.

Disorders and high-yield distinctions

Keep the cause or defining feature attached to each name.

Myasthenia gravis

Autoimmune disorder of the neuromuscular junction causing skeletal-muscle weakness and fatigue.

Muscular dystrophy

Progressive skeletal-muscle degeneration, usually due to a genetic disorder.

Tetany

Rapid muscle spasms associated with low Ca²⁺ levels in body fluid.

Arthritis

Inflammation of joints.

Osteoporosis

Age-associated decrease in bone mass with increased fracture risk; reduced estrogen is a common cause.

Gout

Joint inflammation caused by accumulation of uric-acid crystals.

11

Last-minute revision

Open each checkpoint for rapid recall.

What changes during sarcomere contraction?

Z lines come closer, I bands shorten, H zone narrows or disappears, and overlap increases. A-band length remains constant.

What activates the actin binding sites?

An action potential releases Ca²⁺ from sarcoplasmic reticulum. Ca²⁺ binds troponin, moving the troponin–tropomyosin complex away from actin’s myosin-binding sites.

What are the axial and appendicular skeletons?

Axial: skull, vertebral column, ribs and sternum—80 bones. Appendicular: limb bones plus pectoral and pelvic girdles—126 bones.

Which joint examples are essential?

Fibrous—skull sutures; cartilaginous—adjacent vertebrae; ball and socket—shoulder; hinge—knee; pivot—atlas–axis; gliding—carpals; saddle—thumb.
