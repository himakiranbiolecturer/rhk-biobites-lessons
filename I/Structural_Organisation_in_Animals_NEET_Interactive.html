Framework

Organ & organ system

In multicellular animals, cells form tissues; tissues build organs; organs coordinate as organ systems. This permits division of labour.

  • An organ contains more than one tissue type.
  • Heart includes epithelial, connective, muscular and neural tissues.
  • Morphology studies external form; anatomy studies internal structure.
Exam lens

Do not treat an organ as a collection of identical tissues. Its coordinated tissue types enable a specialised function.

Morphology

Frog profile & skin

Rana tigrina is placed in Amphibia under Chordata. It is poikilothermic: body temperature varies with the environment.

  • Skin is smooth, slippery and kept moist by mucus.
  • Dorsal side: olive green with irregular dark spots.
  • Ventral side: uniformly pale yellow.
  • Water is absorbed through skin; a frog does not drink it.
Exam lens

Camouflage is protective coloration. Aestivation is summer sleep; hibernation is winter sleep.

Morphology

External landmarks

The body has a head and trunk; neck and tail are absent in the adult.

  • Nostrils lie above the mouth.
  • Bulging eyes have a protective nictitating membrane.
  • A visible tympanum receives sound signals.
  • Forelimbs have 4 digits; stronger hindlimbs have 5 webbed digits.
Exam lens

The external ear is absent. The circular tympanum is the visible hearing structure.

Morphology

Sexual dimorphism

Male frogs can be distinguished externally from females.

  • Male: sound-producing vocal sacs.
  • Male: copulatory pad on the first digit of each forelimb.
  • These features are absent in females.
Exam lens

Four forelimb digits occur in both sexes; the pad on the first digit is the differentiating feature.

Digestive

Food route & digestion

The digestive system consists of an alimentary canal and digestive glands. The intestine is relatively short because the frog is carnivorous.

  • Mouth → buccal cavity → pharynx → oesophagus → stomach → intestine → rectum → cloaca.
  • Bilobed tongue captures prey.
  • HCl and gastric juice act in the stomach.
  • Villi and microvilli aid absorption.
Exam lens

Liver makes bile, gall bladder stores it, and pancreas makes pancreatic juice. Bile emulsifies fat; pancreatic enzymes act on carbohydrates and proteins.

Respiratory

Three respiratory surfaces

Gas exchange varies with habitat and activity.

  • In water: skin (cutaneous respiration) by diffusion.
  • On land: buccal cavity, skin and lungs.
  • During aestivation and hibernation: skin.
  • Lungs are elongated, sac-like structures.
Exam lens

Moist skin is not merely protective—it is a respiratory surface and also absorbs water.

Circulatory

Heart, blood & portals

A frog has a closed blood vascular system and a lymphatic system.

  • Heart: two atria and one ventricle, enclosed by pericardium.
  • Sinus venosus opens into the right atrium.
  • Ventricle opens into conus arteriosus.
  • RBCs are nucleated and contain haemoglobin.
Exam lens

Hepatic portal: intestine ↔ liver. Renal portal: lower body ↔ kidney. Lymph lacks RBCs and has fewer proteins than blood.

Excretory

Ureotelic excretion

Kidneys, ureters, cloaca and urinary bladder form the excretory system. The frog chiefly excretes urea.

  • Kidney units: nephrons or uriniferous tubules.
  • Male ureters also convey sperm and are urinogenital ducts.
  • Female ureters and oviducts open separately into the cloaca.
  • Urinary bladder lies ventral to the rectum.
Exam lens

The cloaca is a common chamber for digestive, urinary and reproductive outputs—not a digestive organ alone.

Neural

Coordination & senses

Neural and endocrine systems coordinate body functions. Frog nervous system has CNS, PNS and autonomic divisions.

  • There are 10 pairs of cranial nerves.
  • Forebrain: olfactory lobes, cerebral hemispheres, diencephalon.
  • Midbrain: paired optic lobes.
  • Hindbrain: cerebellum and medulla oblongata.
Exam lens

The ear serves hearing and equilibrium. Receptors include sensory papillae, taste buds, nasal epithelium, eyes and internal ears.

Endocrine

Chemical coordination

Hormones are secreted by endocrine glands and regulate body functions.

  • Pituitary, thyroid, parathyroid, thymus and pineal.
  • Pancreatic islets and adrenals.
  • Gonads also have endocrine roles.
Exam lens

Pancreas is a mixed gland: its islets are endocrine, while pancreatic juice is exocrine.

Male

Male reproductive route

Paired yellowish, ovoid testes attach near the upper kidneys by mesorchium.

  • 10–12 vasa efferentia enter each kidney.
  • They communicate with Bidder’s canal.
  • Then: urinogenital duct → cloaca.
Exam lens

In males, the ureter has dual urinary and reproductive transport roles.

Female & life cycle

External fertilisation

Paired ovaries lie near the kidneys but have no functional connection with them. Oviducts open separately into the cloaca.

  • A mature female may lay about 2500–3000 ova.
  • Fertilisation occurs externally in water.
  • Tadpole larva changes into the adult through metamorphosis.
  • Frogs eat insects and link food chains and food webs.
Exam lens

Female ureters carry urine only; oviducts are separate. Do not copy the male arrangement onto the female.

NEET traps

Twelve corrections worth remembering

Read the wrong idea first, then overwrite it with the precise NCERT-aligned distinction.

Not “frog drinks water”

Water is absorbed through its moist skin.

Not 5 + 5 digits

Forelimb has 4; hindlimb has 5 webbed digits.

Not external pinna

External ear is absent; tympanum is visible.

Not four-chambered

Heart has two atria and one ventricle.

Not non-nucleated RBCs

Frog erythrocytes are nucleated.

Portals are not reversed

Hepatic: gut–liver; renal: lower body–kidney.

Not ammonotelic

Adult frog is ureotelic.

Not only pulmonary

Skin and buccal cavity also exchange gases.

Not one female duct

Ureters and oviducts open separately into cloaca.

Not internal fertilisation

Fertilisation is external and occurs in water.

Not direct development

Tadpole undergoes metamorphosis.

Not 12 cranial pairs

Frog has 10 pairs of cranial nerves.
