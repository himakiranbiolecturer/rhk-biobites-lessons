01

Chapter map

Use this route to connect structure, energy conversion and carbon fixation.

WHERE?

Division of labour

Thylakoid membranes: capture light, split water, transport electrons and make ATP/NADPH.

Stroma: uses ATP and NADPH to reduce CO₂ into carbohydrate.

TWO PHASES

Energy first, carbon next

The photochemical phase supplies ATP and NADPH. The biosynthetic phase uses them; it is not a reaction that must occur in darkness.

NCERT ALERT

Oxygen source

Photosynthetic O₂ comes from the oxidation of water, not from CO₂. Van Niel's inference was later confirmed with isotopic studies.

02

Experiments that built the idea

Remember each scientist by the question their experiment answered.

1770

Priestley

Green plants restore air altered by a burning candle or breathing animal.

1779

Ingenhousz

Sunlight and green parts are required for oxygen evolution.

1854

Julius von Sachs

Green parts form glucose, commonly stored as starch; chlorophyll lies in chloroplasts.

1882

Engelmann

Aerobic bacteria clustered in blue and red regions, producing an early action spectrum.

1931

Van Niel

Generalised photosynthesis using hydrogen donors and inferred that plant oxygen comes from H₂O.

03

Pigments and photosystems

Chlorophyll a is the chief pigment; accessory pigments widen usable wavelengths and protect it.

PRIMARY

Chlorophyll a

The reaction-centre pigment. Its absorption peaks broadly match the action spectrum in blue and red regions.

ACCESSORY

Chlorophyll b

Transfers absorbed energy to chlorophyll a and extends the spectral range used.

ACCESSORY

Carotenoids & xanthophylls

Harvest additional wavelengths and help protect chlorophyll a from photo-oxidation.

Light-harvesting complex

Many pigment molecules are bound to proteins. Most form the antenna; one chlorophyll a molecule forms the reaction centre.

Photosystem II

Reaction centre P680. It works earlier in the Z scheme, oxidises water indirectly and replaces lost electrons.

Photosystem I

Reaction centre P700. It re-energises electrons and can support either non-cyclic or cyclic flow.

04

Electron-flow explorer

Select a mode, then tap a component to trace electron movement and products.

Photolysis

At the inner side of the thylakoid membrane, water supplies electrons to PS II and releases protons into the lumen plus oxygen.

2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂

Proton gradient

Water splitting adds H⁺ to the lumen; electron transport moves more H⁺ inward; NADP⁺ reduction consumes H⁺ on the stromal side.

ATP synthase

H⁺ returns from lumen to stroma through CF₀. The CF₁ head uses this energy to phosphorylate ADP, producing ATP on the stromal side.

05

Calvin cycle: CO₂ to carbohydrate

The cycle runs in the stroma and occurs in every photosynthetic plant, including C₄ plants.

1 · CARBOXYLATION

Fix CO₂

RuBisCO adds CO₂ to the 5-C acceptor RuBP. The unstable 6-C intermediate gives two molecules of 3-PGA.

2 · REDUCTION

Make triose phosphate

ATP and NADPH convert 3-PGA to triose phosphate. Some carbon exits toward sucrose and starch.

3 · REGENERATION

Re-form RuBP

Most triose phosphate is rearranged, and ATP is used to regenerate RuBP so fixation can continue.

NEET stoichiometry

For 1 CO₂: 3 ATP + 2 NADPH. For one net triose phosphate (3 CO₂): 9 ATP + 6 NADPH. For one glucose equivalent (6 CO₂): 18 ATP + 12 NADPH.

06

C₃ and C₄ strategy comparator

C₄ plants spatially concentrate CO₂ around RuBisCO; they do not replace the Calvin cycle.

C₃ PLANT

One main cell type

Mesophyll: RuBisCO fixes CO₂ directly.
First stable product: 3-PGA, a 3-carbon compound.
Hot, dry conditions: stomatal closure lowers CO₂ and favours photorespiration.
Examples: wheat, rice, potato.
C₄ PLANT

Spatial separation

Mesophyll: PEP carboxylase fixes HCO₃⁻; OAA is the first stable product.
Transport: OAA becomes malate/aspartate and moves to bundle sheath.
Bundle sheath: C₄ acid releases CO₂; Calvin cycle operates here.
Return: 3-C compound goes back and PEP is regenerated.
Examples: maize, sugarcane, sorghum.

07

Photorespiration

RuBisCO can act as an oxygenase when O₂ competes successfully with CO₂.

Starting event

RuBP + O₂ forms one 3-PGA and one 2-phosphoglycolate. The latter must be salvaged.

Three organelles

The recovery pathway links the chloroplast, peroxisome and mitochondrion.

Why wasteful?

CO₂ is released, and there is no accompanying synthesis of ATP or NADPH. It lowers net photosynthetic output.

08

Factors affecting photosynthesis

Blackman's law: when several factors influence a process, the rate is limited by the factor nearest its minimum.

Light

Quality, intensity and duration matter. Rate rises with intensity until another factor limits it; excessive light can cause photo-oxidative damage.

Carbon dioxide

Often the major limiting factor. C₃ plants respond more strongly to CO₂ enrichment than C₄ plants because C₄ leaves already concentrate CO₂.

Temperature

Carbon reactions are enzyme-driven and strongly temperature sensitive. Tropical C₄ plants generally have a higher optimum than C₃ plants.

Water

Water stress mainly acts indirectly: stomata close, CO₂ entry falls, leaves may wilt and metabolic activity declines.

Internal factors

Leaf age, chlorophyll content, leaf orientation, stomatal number and internal CO₂ all influence capacity.

Limiting-factor logic

Adding more light cannot raise the rate when CO₂ is limiting. Improving the limiting input changes the curve until another factor becomes limiting.

09

High-yield NEET traps

Short corrections for statements that often look almost right.

“Dark” reaction

It is light-independent only in the direct sense; it depends on ATP and NADPH made in light.

PS II comes first

The numbers reflect discovery order. Electron flow begins at PS II and then reaches PS I.

C₄ still uses C₃ cycle

Initial fixation is C₄, but sugar synthesis proceeds through the Calvin cycle in bundle sheath cells.

PEPcase location

It is active in C₄ mesophyll. Bundle sheath cells are rich in RuBisCO and lack PEPcase in the NCERT model.

Cyclic products

Cyclic flow forms ATP, but neither NADPH nor oxygen.

ATP synthesis side

CF₁ projects toward the stroma; chloroplast ATP is released on the stromal side.

Action vs absorption

Absorption spectrum measures light absorbed by a pigment; action spectrum measures photosynthetic effectiveness.

First product vs acceptor

C₃: acceptor RuBP, first stable product 3-PGA. C₄: acceptor PEP, first stable product OAA.

RuBisCO dual activity

High CO₂ favours carboxylation; high O₂/low CO₂ favours oxygenation.

Last-minute revision

Open only the checkpoints you need.

Where are major events located?

Granal/stromal thylakoids: light reactions; lumen: proton accumulation; stromal side: ATP and NADPH formation; stroma: Calvin cycle; C₄ mesophyll: initial HCO₃⁻ fixation; C₄ bundle sheath: Calvin cycle.

What does each named enzyme do?

PEP carboxylase fixes HCO₃⁻ into OAA; RuBisCO carboxylates or oxygenates RuBP; NADP reductase forms NADPH; ATP synthase uses the proton gradient.

What are the essential numerical facts?

Per CO₂ in Calvin cycle: 3 ATP and 2 NADPH. Per glucose: 18 ATP and 12 NADPH. C₄ concentrating adds 2 ATP equivalents per CO₂.
