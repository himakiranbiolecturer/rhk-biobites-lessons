At an excited region, inner-surface current flows towards the next resting region; the outer-surface return current completes the circuit.

Myelinated fibre

Schwann cells form a myelin sheath interrupted by nodes of Ranvier. Myelin electrically insulates internodes and supports rapid node-to-node conduction.

Unmyelinated fibre

A Schwann cell encloses the axon but does not form a myelin sheath. Depolarisation proceeds continuously along the membrane.

06

Synaptic-transmission explorer

Compare the two synapse types, then walk through a chemical synapse.

Faster · rare

Electrical synapse

Pre- and post-synaptic membranes are extremely close. Current passes directly; transmission resembles conduction along one axon.

Cleft · transmitter

Chemical synapse

A fluid-filled synaptic cleft separates neurons. Neurotransmitter carries the signal and can generate an excitatory or inhibitory response.

08

High-yield NEET traps

These pairs are frequent sources of close distractors.

Afferent versus efferent

Afferent arrives at CNS; efferent exits CNS towards effectors.

Dendrite versus axon

Dendrites conduct towards cell body; axon conducts away towards a synapse or neuromuscular junction.

Resting ion pattern

Axoplasm: high K⁺ and negative proteins, low Na⁺. Outside: high Na⁺ and lower K⁺.

Action potential

Na⁺ permeability and influx depolarise; the later rise in K⁺ permeability restores resting polarity.

Grey versus white matter

Cerebral cortex is grey from cell bodies; inner tracts appear white because of myelin.

Brain stem

Midbrain + pons + medulla—not cerebellum—form the brain stem.

11

Last-minute revision

Open each checkpoint for rapid recall.

Why is the resting neuronal membrane polarised?

It is more permeable to K⁺ than Na⁺, contains trapped negative proteins internally, and maintains gradients through the Na⁺/K⁺ pump moving 3 Na⁺ out for 2 K⁺ in.

How does an action potential travel?

Na⁺ influx reverses polarity locally. Local current depolarises the adjacent resting membrane, while K⁺ efflux restores the previous region.

How does a chemical synapse transmit?

An arriving action potential triggers vesicle fusion and neurotransmitter release. Transmitter binds postsynaptic receptors, opens ion channels and generates an excitatory or inhibitory potential.

What are the major brain divisions?

Forebrain: cerebrum, thalamus, hypothalamus. Midbrain: includes cerebral aqueduct and corpora quadrigemina. Hindbrain: pons, cerebellum and medulla.
