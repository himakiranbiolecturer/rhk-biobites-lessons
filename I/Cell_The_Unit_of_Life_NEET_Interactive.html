Foundation

Cell: the minimum living unit

A complete unicellular organism can exist independently and perform essential life functions. Anything less than a complete cell does not ensure independent living.

  • Unicellular: one cell performs all life activities.
  • Multicellular: cells specialise and divide labour.
  • Cell is structural and functional unit of life.
Exam lens

“Structural” means organisms are built of cells; “functional” means life processes occur through cellular activity.

Cell theory

The modern two-point theory

Schleiden studied plants; Schwann studied animal cells and recognised the plant cell wall as distinctive. Virchow completed the idea.

  • All organisms consist of cells and cell products.
  • All cells arise from pre-existing cells.
  • Omnis cellula e cellula is linked with Virchow.
Exam lens

The first formulation did not explain the origin of new cells; Virchow supplied that missing point.

Overview

Universal and variable features

Both prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells possess plasma membrane, cytoplasm, genetic material and ribosomes. Cell shape often reflects function.

  • Mycoplasma: among the smallest cells.
  • Ostrich egg: largest isolated single cell.
  • Human RBC: about 7 μm diameter.
  • Nerve cells: among the longest cells.
Exam lens

Ribosomes occur in all cells; membrane-bound organelles do not.

Prokaryote

Organisation & genetic material

Bacteria, cyanobacteria, mycoplasma and PPLO are prokaryotic. Their genetic material is not enclosed by a nuclear membrane.

  • Genomic DNA is generally a single circular chromosome.
  • Plasmids are small extra-chromosomal circular DNA.
  • Plasmids may confer antibiotic resistance.
  • Membrane-bound organelles are absent; ribosomes are present.
Exam lens

“No true nucleus” does not mean “no DNA.” Prokaryotes have naked genetic material in the nucleoid region.

Cell envelope

Three protective layers

Most bacterial envelopes have glycocalyx → cell wall → plasma membrane, acting together as one protective unit.

  • Loose glycocalyx = slime layer.
  • Thick, tough glycocalyx = capsule.
  • Wall maintains shape and prevents bursting/collapse.
  • Gram response reflects envelope differences.
Exam lens

Mycoplasma lacks a cell wall. The plasma membrane remains selectively permeable.

Surface structures

Flagella, pili & fimbriae

A bacterial flagellum has filament, hook and basal body. Pili and fimbriae are not locomotory structures.

  • Flagellum: motility.
  • Pili: elongated protein tubules.
  • Fimbriae: short bristles aiding attachment.
  • Cyanobacterial chromatophores contain pigments.
Exam lens

Do not apply the eukaryotic 9+2 plan to a bacterial flagellum; the two structures are fundamentally different.

Ribosomes & inclusions

70S machinery and reserves

Prokaryotic 70S ribosomes consist of 50S and 30S subunits and may form polyribosomes on one mRNA.

  • Polysome: many ribosomes translating one mRNA.
  • Inclusions are not membrane bound.
  • Examples: phosphate, glycogen and cyanophycean granules.
  • Gas vacuoles occur in some photosynthetic bacteria.
Exam lens

Svedberg values describe sedimentation behaviour and are not arithmetically additive.

Plasma membrane

Fluid mosaic model

Singer and Nicolson proposed the accepted fluid mosaic model in 1972. Phospholipids form a bilayer with polar heads outward and non-polar tails inward.

  • Integral proteins are partly or fully embedded.
  • Peripheral proteins lie on the surface.
  • Lateral movement supports membrane fluidity.
  • Fluidity helps growth, junctions, secretion, endocytosis and division.
Exam lens

Human erythrocyte membrane is approximately 52% protein and 40% lipid in NCERT.

Transport

Passive, facilitated & active

Selective permeability regulates exchange. Neutral solutes diffuse down a concentration gradient; water movement by diffusion is osmosis.

  • Simple diffusion: no energy, high → low.
  • Polar molecules use carrier proteins.
  • Active transport: low → high using ATP.
  • NCERT example: Na⁺/K⁺ pump.
Exam lens

Carrier use alone does not make transport active; direction relative to gradient and ATP requirement are decisive.

Cell wall

Wall, lamella & plasmodesmata

The non-living wall outside the membrane gives shape, protection and resistance to infection and undesirable macromolecules.

  • Plant wall: cellulose, hemicellulose, pectins and proteins.
  • Algal wall may include cellulose, galactans, mannans and minerals.
  • Middle lamella: mainly calcium pectate.
  • Plasmodesmata connect neighbouring cytoplasm.
Exam lens

Secondary wall develops on the inner, plasma-membrane side of the primary wall.

Endomembrane

ER → Golgi → lysosome/vacuole

ER, Golgi complex, lysosomes and vacuoles form the functionally coordinated endomembrane system.

  • RER: ribosomes; protein synthesis and secretion.
  • SER: lipids and steroidal hormones.
  • Golgi: modification, sorting and packaging.
  • Lysosome: acidic hydrolytic enzymes.
  • Tonoplast encloses vacuole.
Exam lens

Mitochondria, chloroplasts and peroxisomes are not included in the endomembrane system.

Semi-autonomous

Mitochondria & chloroplasts

Both are double-membrane organelles with circular DNA, RNA, 70S ribosomes and protein-synthetic capacity.

  • Mitochondrial cristae increase inner-membrane area.
  • Matrix contains enzymes and genetic machinery.
  • Chloroplast stroma contains enzymes for carbohydrate/protein synthesis.
  • Thylakoids form grana; stroma lamellae connect grana.
Exam lens

Chlorophyll is in thylakoid membranes, not freely dispersed in stroma.

Movement & support

Cytoskeleton, cilia & centrioles

Microtubules, microfilaments and intermediate filaments support shape and motility.

  • Eukaryotic axoneme: 9 peripheral doublets + 2 central tubules.
  • Cilium/flagellum arises from a basal body.
  • Centriole: 9 peripheral triplet fibrils; no central pair.
  • Centrioles form basal bodies and animal-cell spindle apparatus.
Exam lens

Memorise the contrast: axoneme 9+2 doublets; centriole 9 triplets + 0.

Nucleus

Envelope, nucleolus & chromatin

The nuclear envelope is double, with a 10–50 nm perinuclear space. The outer membrane continues with ER and can bear ribosomes.

  • Nuclear pores exchange RNA and proteins in both directions.
  • Nucleolus is non-membranous and synthesises rRNA.
  • Chromatin contains DNA, histones, non-histone proteins and RNA.
  • Chromosomes become distinct during cell division.
Exam lens

More/larger nucleoli occur in cells actively synthesising protein because ribosome production demand is high.

Chromosomes

Centromere position

The centromere is the primary constriction. Kinetochores are disc-shaped structures beside it, and the centromere holds sister chromatids.

  • Metacentric: equal arms.
  • Submetacentric: one shorter, one longer.
  • Acrocentric: one extremely short, one very long.
  • Telocentric: terminal centromere.
  • Secondary constriction may produce a satellite.
Exam lens

A satellite is associated with a secondary constriction, not the primary centromere.

NEET traps

Fifteen corrections to lock in

Each line replaces a common overgeneralisation with the precise NCERT distinction.

Brown ≠ cell discovery

Brown described the nucleus; Leeuwenhoek saw living cells.

Virchow completed theory

He added origin from pre-existing cells.

Prokaryote ≠ no DNA

DNA is present but lacks a nuclear envelope.

Mycoplasma exception

It lacks the bacterial cell wall.

Pili ≠ motility

NCERT assigns bacterial motility to flagella.

70S ≠ 50 + 30 numerically

S values are sedimentation coefficients.

Carrier ≠ always active

Facilitated movement can remain passive.

Middle lamella

Mainly calcium pectate, not cellulose.

Endomembrane limits

Mitochondria, plastids and peroxisomes are excluded.

Cis receives, trans ships

ER vesicles enter the cis face and leave after processing at trans.

Tonoplast

Membrane of a vacuole—not the plasma membrane.

Chlorophyll location

Embedded in thylakoid membranes.

Axoneme vs centriole

9+2 doublets versus nine triplets with no central pair.

Nucleolus ≠ membrane bound

It is continuous with nucleoplasm and makes rRNA.

Satellite ≠ kinetochore

Satellite follows a secondary constriction.
