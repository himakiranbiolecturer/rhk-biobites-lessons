01 · Big picture

What makes a hormone a hormone?

Hormones are non-nutrient chemical messengers produced in trace amounts. Endocrine glands release them into blood because they have no ducts; only cells carrying the matching receptor respond.

Signal

Source

An endocrine gland or specialised endocrine cell secretes a messenger.

Delivery

Circulation

Blood distributes the hormone, often far beyond its source.

Specificity

Receptor

A target responds only if it carries a compatible receptor.

NCERT lens

The human endocrine system includes hypothalamus, pituitary, pineal, thyroid, parathyroids, thymus, adrenals, endocrine pancreas and gonads.

Do not mix up

Endocrine = ductless, blood-borne signal. Exocrine = product travels through a duct. Pancreas performs both roles.

03 · Master control axis

Hypothalamus → pituitary → target

The hypothalamus connects neural information to endocrine control. Releasing or inhibiting hormones reach the anterior pituitary through portal blood; hypothalamic axons regulate the posterior pituitary directly.

Diabetes mellitus

Prolonged hyperglycaemia can produce glucose and ketone bodies in urine. Insulin therapy is used when insulin deficiency is the cause.

Addison’s disease

Results from insufficient secretion by the adrenal cortex. Do not associate it with the adrenal medulla.

07 · Cellular mechanism

Where is the receptor?

Chemical nature predicts receptor location and the usual signalling route.

Major groups

Peptide/protein hormones, steroids, iodothyronines and amino-acid derivatives.

NEET shortcut

Peptide/protein hormones generally use membrane receptors and second messengers; steroid and thyroid hormones generally regulate gene expression through intracellular receptors.

08 · Precision revision

Frequently tested contrasts

PairCorrect distinction
Prolactin vs oxytocinProlactin supports milk formation; oxytocin supports milk ejection and uterine contraction.
Anterior vs posterior pituitaryAnterior pituitary synthesises several hormones. Posterior pituitary stores/releases hypothalamic ADH and oxytocin.
PTH vs calcitoninPTH raises blood Ca²⁺; calcitonin lowers it.
Adrenal cortex vs medullaCortex produces steroid hormones; medulla produces catecholamines.
Insulin vs glucagonInsulin is hypoglycaemic; glucagon is hyperglycaemic.

10 · Last-minute recall

Quick glossary

Tropic hormone

A hormone acting on another endocrine gland, such as TSH or ACTH.

Hyperglycaemic

Raises blood glucose; glucagon is the key pancreatic example.

Hypoglycaemic

Lowers blood glucose; insulin is the principal example.

Portal circulation

A vessel connection carrying hypothalamic regulatory hormones directly to the anterior pituitary.

Catecholamines

Adrenaline and noradrenaline secreted by the adrenal medulla.

Second messenger

An intracellular relay, such as cAMP, IP₃ or Ca²⁺, triggered after a surface receptor is activated.

Iodothyronines

Iodine-containing thyroid hormones T₃ and T₄.

Islets of Langerhans

Endocrine cell clusters of the pancreas containing α and β cells, among others.
