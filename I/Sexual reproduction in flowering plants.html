One flower, two gametophytes, one seed

A flower houses the male and female reproductive structures. Meiosis creates haploid spores; mitosis develops gametophytes; pollination brings them together; double fertilisation begins embryo and endosperm development.

02 · Male gametophyte

Inside the anther

Select a wall layer. From outside inward, the microsporangium wall has epidermis, endothecium, middle layers and tapetum.

Sporogenous tissue forms microspore mother cells (pollen mother cells).
Microsporogenesis: each diploid mother cell undergoes meiosis to form a tetrad of four haploid microspores.
Pollen development: microspores separate and mature into pollen grains—the male gametophytes.
Pollen wall

Exine

Hard outer wall made of sporopollenin. Germ pores are regions where sporopollenin is absent.

Pollen wall

Intine

Thin inner wall of cellulose and pectin. It extends through a germ pore to form the pollen tube.

03 · Female gametophyte

Ovule to mature embryo sac

Ovule essentials
  • Funicle: stalk attaching ovule to placenta.
  • Hilum: junction of funicle and ovule body.
  • Integuments: protective coverings; leave a pore called micropyle.
  • Chalaza: opposite the micropylar end.
  • Nucellus: tissue enclosing the embryo sac.
Megasporogenesis

A diploid megaspore mother cell undergoes meiosis to produce four haploid megaspores. Commonly, only one remains functional: this is monosporic development.

Three mitoses of the functional megaspore produce eight nuclei, organised into a seven-celled embryo sac.

04 · Pollination

Three routes for pollen transfer

Same flower

Autogamy

Pollen reaches the stigma of the same flower. It requires synchrony of pollen release and stigma receptivity plus close anther–stigma placement.

Same plant

Geitonogamy

Pollen moves between different flowers of the same plant. Functionally cross-pollination, but genetically similar to autogamy.

Different plants

Xenogamy

Pollen moves between flowers of different plants of the same species. It brings genetically different pollen.

Outbreeding devices

Dichogamy

Pollen release and stigma receptivity occur at different times.

Herkogamy

Anther and stigma positions prevent self-pollen contact.

Self-incompatibility

A genetic mechanism rejects self-pollen germination or tube growth.

Unisexuality

Monoecy prevents autogamy but not geitonogamy; dioecy prevents both.

Cleistogamy

Flowers remain closed, ensuring autogamy and assured seed set even without pollinators.

Chasmogamy

Flowers open and expose anthers and stigma to pollinating agents.

05 · Recognition and delivery

Pollen–pistil interaction

A compatible pollen grain lands on a receptive stigma and germinates.
The pollen tube emerges through a germ pore, grows through style tissue and carries two male gametes.
The tube enters the ovule, usually through the micropyle, and then enters a synergid through its filiform apparatus.
The pollen tube releases both male gametes into the embryo sac.
Recognition is active

The pistil can recognise compatible and incompatible pollen. An incompatible grain may be prevented from germinating or its tube may be stopped.

Artificial hybridisation

Emasculation removes anthers before dehiscence from a bisexual female-parent flower. Bagging prevents unwanted pollen contamination. Unisexual female flowers need bagging, not emasculation.

06 · Signature event

Build double fertilisation

Select an event to reveal its nuclei, ploidy and product.

Classic trap

The primary endosperm nucleus is usually triploid (3n), while the zygote is diploid (2n). Two separate fusions using the two male gametes together constitute double fertilisation.

Dicot embryo

Embryonal axis bears two cotyledons. Epicotyl ends in plumule; hypocotyl ends in radicle, protected by root cap.

Monocot embryo

Single cotyledon is the scutellum. Plumule is protected by coleoptile and radicle by coleorhiza.

Endosperm

Coconut water is free-nuclear endosperm; white kernel is cellular endosperm. Albuminous seeds retain endosperm at maturity; non-albuminous seeds use it up.

Fruit types

Fruit formed only from ovary is a true fruit. Participation of another floral part produces a false fruit. Fruit formed without fertilisation is parthenocarpic.

08 · Variations

Apomixis and polyembryony

No fertilisation

Apomixis

Seed formation without fertilisation. In some species a diploid egg forms without meiosis, or nucellar cells surrounding the embryo sac develop into embryos.

Crop value: hybrid characters can be maintained across generations if hybrid seed is produced apomictically.

More than one embryo

Polyembryony

Occurrence of more than one embryo in a seed. Citrus and mango may develop additional nucellar embryos.

Relationship: nucellar embryony is an apomictic route and can create polyembryony.

11 · Rapid glossary

Last-minute definitions

Microsporogenesis

Formation of haploid microspores from diploid pollen mother cells by meiosis.

Megasporogenesis

Formation of haploid megaspores from a diploid megaspore mother cell by meiosis.

Sporopollenin

Highly resistant material forming the pollen exine.

Filiform apparatus

Special thickening in synergids that helps guide the pollen tube.

Syngamy

Fusion of one male gamete with the egg to form a diploid zygote.

Triple fusion

Fusion of the other male gamete with two polar nuclei, producing a triploid primary endosperm nucleus.

Parthenocarpy

Fruit formation without fertilisation.

Apomixis

Seed formation without fertilisation.
