01

Chapter map

Respiration is cellular oxidation; breathing or gas exchange only supplies and removes gases.

CORE IDEA

Controlled release

Cells oxidise substrates through enzyme-controlled steps instead of one explosive reaction. Energy is captured mainly in ATP and reduced coenzymes.

WHERE?

Cytosol + mitochondrion

Glycolysis occurs in cytoplasm. Pyruvate oxidation and TCA occur in the matrix; ETS and ATP synthase lie on the inner mitochondrial membrane.

NCERT ALERT

Plants respire continuously

Photosynthesis is restricted to green cells in light, but respiration occurs in all living plant cells, during day and night.

02

How plants exchange gases

Plants need no specialised respiratory organs because diffusion distances and respiratory demand are generally low.

Leaves

Stomata and interconnected air spaces permit diffusion. Each leaf largely manages its own exchange.

Woody stems

Lenticels connect living layers beneath the bark with outside air; central supporting tissues are often dead.

Roots

O₂ diffuses from air spaces in soil into living root cells. Waterlogging can restrict oxygen availability.

04

Glycolysis · EMP pathway

Ten cytosolic reactions partially oxidise one glucose into two pyruvate molecules.

INVESTMENT

2 ATP used

ATP phosphorylates glucose to glucose-6-phosphate and fructose-6-phosphate to fructose-1,6-bisphosphate.

CLEAVAGE

Two 3-C units

Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate splits into DHAP and PGAL; DHAP is converted so two PGAL molecules continue.

PAYOFF

4 ATP + 2 NADH

Substrate-level phosphorylation occurs twice per triose. Net outcome: 2 ATP, 2 NADH and 2 pyruvate.

06

Aerobic respiration inside mitochondria

Matrix reactions remove carbon and hydrogen; the inner membrane converts electron energy into ATP.

LINK REACTION

Pyruvate → acetyl-CoA

Each pyruvate undergoes oxidative decarboxylation by pyruvate dehydrogenase: CO₂ and NADH form while the 2-C acetyl group joins CoA.

TCA CYCLE

Acetyl group fully oxidised

Acetyl-CoA + OAA forms citrate. Per turn: 2 CO₂, 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂ and 1 GTP/ATP; OAA is regenerated.

ETS

Electrons reach O₂

NADH donates at complex I and FADH₂ at complex II. Electrons pass via ubiquinone, complex III, cytochrome c and complex IV.

Proton gradient

Electron transfer drives H⁺ from matrix to intermembrane space. Their return through F₀ powers ATP formation at F₁.

Oxidative phosphorylation

Redox energy, rather than light, establishes the gradient. O₂ accepts terminal electrons and hydrogen to form water.

Substrate-level exceptions

Direct ATP/GTP formation occurs in glycolysis and at succinyl-CoA → succinate, independent of the proton gradient.

08

Why respiration is amphibolic

Its intermediates participate in both breakdown and biosynthesis.

Fats enter

Glycerol can enter after conversion to PGAL; fatty acids are degraded to acetyl-CoA.

Proteins enter

After deamination, amino-acid carbon skeletons can enter as pyruvate, acetyl-CoA or TCA intermediates.

Intermediates leave

Acetyl-CoA and TCA intermediates may be withdrawn for synthesis of fatty acids, amino acids and other molecules—making the pathway both catabolic and anabolic.

10

High-yield NEET traps

Corrections to statements that are almost—but not quite—right.

Glycolysis is universal

It occurs in the cytoplasm of aerobic and anaerobic organisms; oxygen is not directly used.

Fermentation ATP

Fermentation itself mainly regenerates NAD⁺. Its net 2 ATP come from glycolysis.

O₂ acts last

It is vital but serves directly only as the terminal acceptor in the ETS.

CO₂ is not from glycolysis

It is released during pyruvate oxidation, TCA decarboxylations and alcoholic fermentation—not glycolysis or lactic fermentation.

Complex II difference

FADH₂ enters through complex II and yields fewer ATP under the NCERT convention because it bypasses complex I.

38 ATP is conditional

NCERT explicitly treats the balance sheet as a theoretical exercise based on simplifying assumptions.

F₀ versus F₁

F₀ forms the proton channel; F₁ contains the catalytic sites for ATP synthesis.

TCA starts and ends with OAA

OAA accepts acetyl-CoA to form citrate and is regenerated at the end.

RQ depends on substrate

RQ is not an efficiency score. It reports the CO₂/O₂ gas ratio.

Last-minute revision

Open a checkpoint for a rapid recall.

What happens per glucose before ETS?

Glycolysis: 2 pyruvate, net 2 ATP and 2 NADH. Link reaction: 2 acetyl-CoA, 2 CO₂ and 2 NADH. Two TCA turns: 4 CO₂, 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂ and 2 GTP/ATP.

Where do respiratory events occur?

Cytosol—glycolysis and fermentation; mitochondrial matrix—pyruvate oxidation and TCA cycle; inner mitochondrial membrane—ETS and ATP synthase; intermembrane space—proton accumulation.

Which events make ATP directly?

Substrate-level phosphorylation occurs twice in glycolysis and during succinyl-CoA to succinate. Most aerobic ATP comes through oxidative phosphorylation.
